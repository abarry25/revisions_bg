Revision passes

How to use this. Do the passes in order. Each has an entry condition, a fixed scope, and an exit condition. Do not let a later pass bleed into an earlier one — the sequence exists because Pass 3 changes what the sentences in Pass 7 need to do, and Pass 0 changes what Pass 3 is for. Every item is graded Small / Medium / Large; pick one level per item and move on.

Three questions to carry through every pass

  1. Does this scene put pressure on Maeren's invisibility, or let her keep it? Book One's emotional question is can Maeren remain invisible and keep the people she loves safe — and the answer is no. Every scene should be testable against that.
  2. Is magic pointing, or deciding? Magic can say LOOK. Maeren decides I will look.
  3. Can the reader tell when her silence, observation, mercy, restraint, or surrender is a decision? Not "is she active enough" — that question is now answered.

Pass 0 · Decide

One sitting · No drafting. Write the answers down where you can see them. Everything downstream depends on these three — two of them change what sentences have to do; the third gates a find-and-replace.

0.1 · Is the transformation culmination or regression?

Recommendation: regression. The manuscript already argues this. At ch. 68 (p. 424) Maeren takes hold of a power that is visibly sick — "It weakly pulled back as if sick" — overrides its resistance, and commands it: "lit the black fire within her and demanded it obey her." She is doing exactly what the Order does to the druids and the Elders do to the rain. And she is breaking the only rule she was given, Kēlē at ch. 49 (p. 285): "When you feel that resistance, listen to it… If you fight it, it will drain you and the consequences will potentially be irreversible."

The wall she builds at the Veil is her chapter 1 belief at full strength. "It's not worth the risk" (ch. 1) → "They would not protect her. She would not let them in" (ch. 68).

If you confirm regression, three things become obligatory — they are Passes 3 and 6:

  • Maeren must name what the storm taught her, or she cannot be seen to abandon it.
  • The wall at the Veil should rhyme on the page with ch. 1.
  • She must be present, at length, for her own fall.

0.2 · Who decodes the amulet?

Three options. Pick one before Pass 3.

The clue is already planted and marked at ch. 48 (p. 271): "'My mother gave it to me,' Maeren said quietly… 'As protection.' Protection. The word landed heavily." The manuscript stops there. Option C picks it back up.

0.3 · Name and orthography rulings

Decide now; Pass 2 executes.

Maevira or Minvaera? Maevira is used in the creation myth and the prophecy and is presumably canonical. Minvaera appears 6×, Minevaera 1×, Mineveara 1× — including in a chapter title (Minvaera Burns, ch. 67, p. 415) and in Cydar's self-introduction (p. 417): "I am a druid from Minevaera's Grove." Meanwhile ch. 42 is titled Maevira's Grove.

Astraen or Astrean? The prophecy says "A Bear, of the blood of Astraen and Maevira" (pp. 57, 71, 231, 408). The prose says Astrean 26× including every oath ("So is the will of Astrean," p. 239). A reader will assume two gods.

Accents: in or out? Rían loses its accent 80× of 504 (16%). Orlíen loses it 95× of 134 — the unaccented form is now dominant. Diré loses it 4× of 21. Commit or drop. Half-commitment is the only wrong answer.

Pass 7 · Line Pass

The long one · gate: all drafting done. Only after all drafting is finished.

7.1 · The filter sweep — the highest-value line work in the book

Problem. Perception filters run at 50 per 10,000 words: could see/feel/hear/sense (173), seemed (89), saw (64), started to (64), watched (59), began to (34), plus as if / as though (122) and like a (56). Each one inserts the act of perceiving between the reader and the thing perceived. "She could see the flecks of shell" is one remove further than "Flecks of shell caught the light." You already do it right in her best scenes: the two-body deduction (ch. 7), reading Alai's body (ch. 8), the map discrepancy (ch. 59). None of those use "could see." Expect to cut 1–2% of total wordcount.

Small
Sweep could see / could feel (173) and delete the construction wherever the object is concrete. "She could see the arrow had struck between the neck and right arm" → "The arrow had struck between the neck and right arm."
Medium
Add seemed (89). Roughly half are genuine POV uncertainty; the other half hedge things that simply are.
Large
Take the ten most filtered paragraphs in Maeren's POV and rewrite them with zero perception verbs. Diagnostic even where you revert.

7.2 · Body parts acting alone

Problem. 118 instances of her/his [body part] + past-tense verb as an independent actor, eyes alone accounting for 43. Around 105 per 10,000 words in every POV. In a novel whose central craft problem has been whether Maeren's actions read as chosen, a narration in which her stomach, eyes, heart and lungs keep acting without her works against you at sentence level.

Small
Cap the eyes constructions. Forty-three instances of eyes darting, flicking, narrowing and widening is enough for three books.
Medium
Do this only in the decision beats — the fifteen turns in the agency ledger. Make Maeren the subject of the sentence where she decides; let the body react in the sentence after, not during.
Large
Make it a system. Dense when she is overwhelmed, absent when she is resolved. That turns a tic into grammar.

7.3 · Gesture vocabulary

Problem. 557 beats, one per ~196 words — fine in volume, top-heavy in distribution: stare (80), head-shaking (48), sigh (47), tilt (35), nod (31), flinch (30), shrug (26).

Small
Cut the weakest third of the stare and head-shake beats. Most dialogue survives with no beat at all.
Medium
Give each POV three or four signature habits and stop sharing them. Brynna cracks her neck, grips a hilt. Aurelian's hands go to soil. Maeren wrings her hands — you already have this, and Eamon notices it (ch. 4). Rían rubs the bridge of his nose.
Large
In scenes of conflicting want, replace beats with action that advances the conflict.

7.4 · Four points of view, one voice

Problem. Measured across all four POVs: mean sentence length 11.2–12.4 · fragments 12–17% · as if 9.5–13.7/10k · adverbs 125–137/10k · body-part subjects 102–115/10k. Statistically indistinguishable. The one thing already working, and you did not engineer it: Brynna asks the fewest questions in the book (28 per 10k), Rían the most (52). She acts because she is certain; he has stopped being able to answer anything.

Small
Strip the filter constructions from Brynna entirely. She does not perceive; she registers and acts. This single subtraction separates her chapters from everyone else's immediately, and it is the cheapest differentiation available.
Medium
Differentiate by syntax. Brynna in short declaratives and imperatives, minimal subordination, no hedging. Aurelian in longer self-interrupting sentences that circle back. Maeren in accumulating observation with the conclusion at the end. Rían in questions and negations.
Large
Assign each POV a metaphor domain. Aurelian's should be botanical — it is his expertise and he barely uses it. Maeren's should be textile: thread, weave, seam. You already reach for it — "the fraying seam where the mystery of the bear… converged" (ch. 8).

7.5 · Two smaller line notes

Sentence variety. Only 2.4% of sentences run 29+ words. You write almost entirely in the 6–18 band and have very few sentences that build, subordinate and release. There is no slow gear. Small: in the three or four most reflective passages, let one sentence run long.

Em dashes. Five in 109,000 words. You have effectively no interruptive punctuation — which is the natural tool for the self-interrupting interiority Aurelian's chapters keep reaching for. Decide whether that absence is a choice.

🌍 World canon — Book Two/Three parking lot

The rot & magical imbalance

  • The shadow is not an evil alternate transformation or a separate force attacking Maeren — it's magic rotting from within.
  • The Shai'thi are creating unnatural additional Veils because they can no longer access their power; these artificial Veils distort the flow of magic between realms, and the imbalance shows up as blight, rot, and magic deteriorating from within.
  • Maeren is especially vulnerable as the core of the Bear Guardian system — like frostbite, the core may survive but the damage has already spread through the tendrils.

The amulet (background)

  • Developed by the Arios and passed to the firstborn daughter each generation to prevent the Bear Guardian from transforming. The original reason is lost to time.
  • The amulet was never protecting Maeren from the shadow — its suppression of the transformation is part of what let the magic rot in the first place. (See Pass 0.2 for the Book One decision on how this plays out.)

Book Two opening

  • Opens with Maeren visibly deteriorated — when Rían finds her near Seradon, roughly half her face is shadow.
  • This leads to Aurelian's sacrifice, interrupting the generational pattern of women consumed by duty.

Book Three direction

  • Book One's Veil closure was a desperate survival act, not a solution. The prophecy's fuller meaning involves ripping open the seams between the Veils so the realms can reemerge and the original wound can heal.

Vaela, the Fions & the Battle of Belmora (Book Two)

  • Vaela stays in osprey form throughout the battle; her screeches signal Brynna's formations. She flies into Maeren's storm on arrival and emerges through it in front of Brynna, who already knows Vaela is a Fion.
  • Open question: scale of the reveal when Vaela transforms afterward — decide when this book is drafted.

Ask beta readers — don't preemptively fix

  • Does Astralis feel too long?
  • Do the two realms feel disconnected while the characters are separated?
  • Does Maeren feel agentic?
  • Does the unresolved Maeren/Brynna relationship feel intentional?
  • Does Rían earn the betrayal, or does it feel unearned?
  • Is Aurelian sympathetic enough to follow?
  • Is the lore comprehensible?
  • Does the ending create Book Two desire?
  • Which mysteries are predictable?
  • Which emotional moments land hardest?
